# Runibex

Runibex is a technology products company, founded in 1988, that builds and operates commercial software products on public cloud infrastructure. We run our own products in production on AWS and Azure, and we provide the cloud engineering and managed services that came out of doing that work. We serve enterprises across Europe and the Middle East from offices in Istanbul and London.

Our commercial model shapes the technical advice we give, so it is worth being direct about it. Runibex is paid by the customer, not by cloud vendors. There are no consumption kickbacks or volume incentives in our revenue. Recommending that a customer reduce their cloud spend, move a workload between providers, or keep something on-premises costs us nothing. We hold partnerships with AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Huawei Cloud, and we are commercially independent of all three.

Our cloud expertise is a byproduct of building and running production systems for years rather than a separate consulting practice. When we advise on architecture, FinOps, or operations, it is from the position of a team that operates these platforms daily.

## Products

All products are developed and owned by Runibex.

### NGI Commerce Suite

An enterprise commerce operating system. Composable, API-first, and cloud-native, it runs direct-to-consumer, B2B, B2B2C, omnichannel, and configure-price-quote (CPQ) commerce on one operational core, so adding a commerce model is a composition step rather than a re-platforming project.

Six capability clusters share a unified data model: Commerce Core (order management, product information management, pricing, campaigns, checkout), Experience and Personalisation, Partner and Channel Management, the ConnectBus integration backbone, Data/Intelligence/Security, and Fulfilment and Logistics.

ConnectBus is a commerce-native integration backbone built into the suite rather than third-party middleware. It uses a hub-and-spoke architecture with pre-built connectors for ERP, CRM, POS, WMS, payment processors, logistics carriers, and marketplaces, so systems connect to one hub instead of to each other. It supports REST and GraphQL APIs, webhooks, and event streaming, with data validation, transformation, monitoring, and retry logic. Every integration feeds the same unified data model.

Partner and channel management is native to the product, covering partner roles and permissions, dedicated partner portals, onboarding workflows, and pricing and inventory guardrails enforced automatically. Fully managed on AWS and Azure.

### Centrigate

A commercial control layer for cloud and digital subscriptions. Centrigate sits between vendor systems and business operations to govern renewals, pricing, margins, consumption alignment, and order-to-cash across multi-vendor portfolios. It is built for cloud distributors and aggregators, cloud service providers and data centre operators, and managed service providers and resellers.

Seven capability clusters cover cloud and digital asset aggregation, marketplace and channel enablement, subscription lifecycle management, consumption and usage management, pricing and margin governance, order-to-cash automation, and an integration and continuity layer.

Current vendor integrations include Microsoft, Acronis, and Huawei Cloud, with an architecture designed for incremental vendor onboarding so adding a vendor does not require rebuilding existing connections. Bidirectional ERP and finance integration covers partner and customer data, orders, invoices, and financial documents. Alongside software subscriptions and digital licences, Centrigate handles physical product sales and rental under the same commercial model.

Centrigate is not a vendor portal, a marketplace, or a billing tool. It connects and governs the commercial lifecycle across the systems a business already runs.

### Runibex Field Management

A field operations management platform for daily site execution. It distributes work orders to crews and individuals, enforces job-specific safety surveys and checklists, captures timestamped worklogs with photo and video evidence, tracks materials and equipment time against the work that consumed them, and delivers same-day consolidated reporting to planning and project management offices.

It is offline-first by design rather than offline-capable as a fallback. Full data capture works in basements, tunnels, and remote sites, with automatic synchronisation when connectivity returns and no change to the workflow or data quality in the meantime.

Eight capability pillars cover safety and compliance, work orders and execution control, surveys and guided workflows with conditional logic, worklogs and evidence capture, materials and equipment tracking, IoT and flowmeter integration for high-variance consumables, reporting and payment measurement, and offline execution and synchronisation. It integrates with project management and ERP systems over APIs, with bidirectional synchronisation of work orders, crew assignments, and equipment requirements.

Primary users are general contractors and prime organisations running multi-crew delivery, specialist subcontractors in repair, rehabilitation, and maintenance work, and inspection and survey teams.

## Cloud engineering and managed services

Runibex Cloud Services is a 28-service portfolio spanning cloud operations, modernisation, and data work. Services group into nine capability areas: SAP Services, Cloud Managed Services, Transformation Readiness, Application Modernisation and DevOps, Back-Office and Knowledge Automation, Industrial and Operational Intelligence, Sales/Marketing/Revenue Intelligence, Customer Experience and Engagement, and Cloud and Software Commerce.

The work most relevant to engineering teams:

**SAP operations.** Hosting and operations, Basis management, HANA optimisation, and SAP Business One. Operational runbooks, change governance, kernel and patch management, and high-availability and disaster-recovery architecture, with 7/24 coverage for critical workloads.

**Cloud managed services.** Cloud environment management and landing zone design, DevOps management, database management, monitoring/observability/incident response (MOIR), and backup and disaster recovery with tested drills measured against RTO and RPO targets. Reliability is expressed as SLOs and SLIs rather than uptime claims.

**FinOps.** Cost allocation models, showback and chargeback, tagging compliance, and unit economics. This is the most common entry point for organisations that have finished migrating and cannot connect the cloud bill to business outcomes.

**Delivery modernisation.** Golden path standards for repositories, pipelines, and quality gates, CI/CD design, security-by-design gates, and DORA metric baselines.

**Assessments.** Fixed-fee reviews of two to four weeks covering FinOps, infrastructure modernisation, DevSecOps, and AI and data readiness. Output is a maturity scorecard, a gap list, and a 30/60/90 roadmap.

Every service follows the same delivery mechanism: discover, design, pilot, operate, scale. KPIs and a continuous service improvement backlog are part of each engagement.

### Engagement modes

There are three, and customers can enter at any of them.

**Advisory.** Vendor-neutral assessment and recommendations, typically two to four weeks on a fixed fee. The output is a roadmap and a recommendation rather than a dependency.

**Project-based.** Defined scope and defined outcome. Migrations, data platform builds, architecture redesign, DevOps modernisation, and co-development engagements. Typically four to twenty weeks, fixed price or time-and-materials against milestones.

**Managed services.** Ongoing infrastructure and platform operation against SLOs, covering incident response, DR testing, database health, pipeline management, and FinOps governance. Monthly recurring, tiered by scope and SLA.

Most relationships start with advisory and expand as value is demonstrated.

### Embedded engineering

Several of our products began as customer co-development. The pattern is that we work inside the problem rather than against a fixed specification: discovering requirements jointly, contributing cloud-native architecture, security-by-design, and operational discipline, and building something intended to keep evolving after launch.

The customer owns the product and the codebase. Runibex contributes the engineering, the cloud platform, and the experience of having shipped and operated products on it before.

## Open source

Our products are commercial and closed-source. NGI Commerce Suite, Centrigate, and Runibex Field Management are licensed and operated for customers rather than published here.

This organisation is where our public work lives. It is early and deliberately small at the moment. We expect it to grow as we extract pieces that are genuinely useful outside the context they were built in: infrastructure modules, integration tooling, and reference implementations. We would rather maintain a few repositories properly than publish many and abandon them.

Where we use open source in our own stack, which is most of it, we contribute back where the work is separable from customer-specific code and where we can commit to maintaining it.

If you are evaluating Runibex technically, a conversation with the engineers who would work on your account will tell you more than a repository tour at this stage. We are happy to arrange that.
